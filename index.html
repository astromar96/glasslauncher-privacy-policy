Glass Launcher — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 September 2026

Glass Launcher is a home screen replacement for Android, published by Pixelz Apps. This policy explains exactly what the app does with data on your device, and what leaves it.

Glass Launcher has no accounts, and the developer operates no server. Nothing you do in the launcher is sold. Most of what the app reads never leaves your phone; the few things that do go to the services named below, each for one purpose.

What stays on your device

All of the following is read and used entirely on your phone. None of it is transmitted anywhere, and the developer has no way to see it:

DataWhy the app reads it
Installed apps and their icons To draw the app grid, App Library and Search. This is what a launcher is.
Notifications (notification access) Optional, and off unless you grant it in Android Settings. Lets the Dynamic Island show what is playing, a timer or a call, and lets icons show badges. Notifications are read to be displayed and are never stored or sent.
Phone calls (ANSWER_PHONE_CALLS) Optional. Lets you answer or end a ringing call from the Dynamic Island.
Calendar events (READ_CALENDAR) Optional. Shows your next event on the clock widget. If you decline the permission, the line is hidden.
App usage history (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) Optional, and off unless you grant it in Android Settings. Ranks Suggestions by what you have actually been using. Without it, Suggestions falls back to install date.
Photos you choose as a wallpaper Copied into the app's private storage so the home screen can draw them.
Your home screen layout and preferences Icon positions, folders, pages, widgets, hidden apps and settings, in the app's own private storage. A backup is written only to a folder you choose.

Accessibility services

Glass Launcher includes two optional accessibility services, each off until you turn it on in Android Settings:

  • Dynamic Island and edge light uses the accessibility service to draw the island and the edge light above other apps and the status bar.
  • Lock screen uses it to lock your phone when you double-tap the home screen.

Neither service reads the contents of your screen, receives accessibility events, or performs actions on other apps. They are used only to draw and to lock.

What leaves your device

ServiceWhat is sent, and when
Google Analytics for Firebase How the app is used, as a short list of fixed events: which screens are opened, which settings and features are chosen (for example, which icon style or kind of widget), and whether setup steps are completed. Also the device model, Android version, app version, country and an app-instance identifier that Firebase creates.
Firebase Crashlytics When the app crashes or stops responding: the technical details of the failure, the device model and Android version, and a few app settings in effect at the time (such as the glass quality level and whether the Dynamic Island is on).
Google AdMob The free version shows ads from Google AdMob, which receives device information and, where you allow it, the advertising ID, to show and measure ads. Glass Pro removes the ads.
Open-Meteo Only if you add the weather widget and grant approximate location: your latitude and longitude, rounded to two decimal places (roughly a one-kilometre area), to fetch a forecast. No identifier is attached, and location is never used in the background.
Pixabay Only if you browse or search the online wallpaper gallery: the words of your search, to find images, which are then downloaded from Pixabay.
Google Play Buying Glass Pro happens entirely in Google Play. The app only learns whether Glass Pro is owned; it never sees payment details.

What is never sent

Glass Launcher never sends the list of apps you have installed or any app's name, the content of your notifications or which app posted them, anything you type (searches, folder or app names) to its analytics, your photos, your calendar, your precise location, or details of your calls. Analytics never uses the advertising ID.

Your choices

  • Share usage and crash reports, on the About page of the Control Center, is on by default. Turning it off stops both Analytics and Crashlytics from sending anything.
  • In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks you before anything is used for ads or analytics, and Analytics uses no identifier for your device unless you consent. Privacy choices, on the same About page, lets you change your answer at any time. Crash reports are sent on the developer's legitimate interest in fixing crashes, and stop when you turn the switch above off.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings, under Privacy → Ads.

Google processes this data as a service provider under its own terms. See Privacy and Security in Firebase and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

Permissions, and what happens if you refuse

Every permission is optional, and every one degrades to silence rather than to a guess:

  • Location refused → the weather tile says so and shows no temperature.
  • Calendar refused → the agenda line is hidden entirely.
  • Usage access not granted → Suggestions ranks by install date.
  • Notification access not granted → the Dynamic Island and badges stay empty.
  • Phone calls refused → the island shows a call but cannot answer it.

The app does not request contacts, SMS, camera, microphone, precise location or background location permissions.

Children

Glass Launcher is not directed at children and is declared for users aged 18 and over. It does not knowingly collect data from children.

Data retention and deletion

Everything on your device lives in Android's private storage for this app. Uninstalling Glass Launcher, or clearing its storage in Android Settings, permanently deletes it. Analytics and crash data are kept by Google only for a limited period, are not linked to your name or account, and stop being collected when you turn off Share usage and crash reports.
